# Agency AI terms

Free templates for marketing, creative and digital agencies (and freelancers) whose clients ask how they use AI:

- **[client-ai-statement.md](client-ai-statement.md)**: a plain-language note you send a client before they ask. Where you use AI, who checks every deliverable, what happens to their data, who owns the result.
- **[ai-addendum.md](ai-addendum.md)**: an AI addendum for your master services agreement or statement of work. Ten numbered clauses: definitions, permitted use, human review, confidential information and personal data, generated media, autonomous agent actions, ownership, fees, client AI policy, liability.

Both files list every option. Delete what does not apply to you, or use the **[AI terms generator](https://bigpipe.agency/agency-ai-terms-generator?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=ai-terms&utm_content=readme)** to tick where you actually use AI and get a version with only those parts (copy or download, free, no signup).

## Why send AI terms first

In a World Federation of Advertisers survey of 48 of the world's biggest advertisers (September 2024), 80% had concerns about how their agencies use generative AI, and only 36% had introduced terms on how partners may use it on their behalf. An agency that arrives with a written standard answers the question before it becomes a negotiation, including a request for an [AI discount](https://bigpipe.agency/blog/clients-want-an-ai-discount-what-to-say?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=ai-terms&utm_content=readme-discount).

## How many agencies publish one?

Very few. In October 2026 we read the websites of 104 small marketing agencies (2 to 10 people, US and UK): 28% sold AI on their homepage, 6% said anywhere in writing how they use it, and 2% published an AI policy page. Five of the seven listed agency holding groups publish written AI commitments. The counts, the method and the policies worth copying are in [research/agency-ai-policy-audit-2026-10.md](research/agency-ai-policy-audit-2026-10.md) and the full write-up, [Agency AI policy examples](https://bigpipe.agency/blog/agency-ai-policy-examples?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=ai-terms&utm_content=readme-audit).

## What the templates commit you to

| Topic | Client statement | Contract addendum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where AI is used | Listed in plain words | Clause 2, a closed list of purposes; anything else needs written consent |
| Human review | A named role approves every deliverable | Clause 3, review of facts, sources and brand fit before delivery or publication |
| Client data | Never in an AI tool without written consent (strictest), or business tools that do not train on it | Clause 4, with the alternative wording |
| Images, video, voice | Disclosed, commercially licensed tools only, no imitation of real people | Clause 5 (optional) |
| AI agents on client accounts | Written limits, a person monitors, stopped on request | Clause 6 (optional) |
| Ownership | Same as any deliverable, with the copyright caveat | Clause 7 |
| Fees | Priced by deliverable, not hours (optional) | Clause 8 (optional) |
| The client's own AI policy | Followed | Clause 9 |

On ownership: the U.S. Copyright Office concluded in January 2025 that AI output is protectable only where a human author has determined sufficient expressive elements, and that prompts alone are not enough. The templates say so rather than promising copyright in raw AI output.
